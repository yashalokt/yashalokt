How it works

Four steps from "is a charger free?" to charging.

This is what a resident does. No calls, no messages, no waiting in the basement.

  1. Join the queue

    Open EVNest, choose how long you need, and tap Join. You see your place in line and when you'll likely start.

  2. Get your turn

    When a charger frees up, you get a notification. The charger is held for you for 10 minutes.

  3. Plug in and charge

    Scan the QR code on the charger to start. Watch progress, units and cost live on your phone.

  4. Unplug and go

    You get a reminder when charging is done. The charger goes to the next person automatically.

Features

Everything a society needs to share a few chargers.

EVNest is built for the common case: two to ten chargers in the parking, and many more cars than that.

Fair queue

First come, first served across all chargers. Start times update when someone finishes early or runs late.

Plug-in hold

The next person gets a short window to plug in. If they don't come, the charger passes to the next flat.

Default: 10 min

"Car is full" alerts

Owners get a nudge when charging ends. Societies can add a small idle fee for cars left plugged in.

e.g. ₹2/min after 15 min

Bill for each flat

Every session is measured in kWh and priced at your society's rate. Totals go straight to the maintenance bill.

Safe power use

Keeps the total power drawn within your building's sanctioned load, so the main supply never trips.

e.g. 3 × 7.4 kW = 22.2 kW

Overnight booking

Book a charger for the night ahead of time. Use cheaper night rates where your electricity board offers them.

FAQ

Common questions

What is EVNest?

EVNest is an app for housing societies that share a few EV chargers among many residents. It runs a fair queue, tells you when it's your turn, and bills each flat for the electricity it used.

Do we need to buy new chargers?

No. EVNest is designed to work with the AC chargers societies commonly install, such as 3.3 kW and 7.4 kW wall boxes.

What if someone doesn't come when it's their turn?

The charger is held for 10 minutes (your committee can change this). If they don't plug in, it automatically goes to the next person in line.

What if a car stays plugged in after it's full?

The owner gets a reminder when charging ends. Societies can also turn on a small idle fee for cars that stay plugged in too long.

How is the electricity bill split?

Each charging session is measured in units (kWh) and multiplied by the rate your committee sets. At the end of the month, each flat's total is ready to add to its maintenance bill.

Who can use the chargers?

Only residents the committee has approved. Each flat registers its EV before it can join the queue.
